📌 Overview

Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS) is a disorder of the autonomic nervous system characterized by an excessive increase in heart rate when standing, accompanied by symptoms of orthostatic intolerance.

POTS can affect daily activities, education, employment, and quality of life. Its symptoms may include dizziness, palpitations, fatigue, exercise intolerance, and difficulty concentrating.

This project provides educational resources about POTS, with a focus on nursing practice, patient education, medical research, and digital health innovation.

Our mission: Make reliable health information more accessible through technology, education, and evidence-informed healthcare communication.

🎯 Project Objectives

🧠 Understanding POTS

Common Symptoms

People with POTS may experience:

- Tachycardia when standing.
- Dizziness and lightheadedness.
- Palpitations.
- Persistent fatigue.
- Brain fog and difficulty concentrating.
- Exercise intolerance.
- Shortness of breath.
- Digestive symptoms.
- Heat intolerance.

Symptoms vary between individuals and may significantly affect daily functioning.

Diagnostic Considerations

POTS is diagnosed through clinical evaluation.

Common diagnostic criteria include:

- A sustained heart-rate increase of at least 30 beats per minute within 10 minutes of standing in adults.
- A threshold of at least 40 beats per minute for adolescents aged 12–19 years.
- Symptoms of orthostatic intolerance that worsen upright and improve when lying down.
- Absence of significant orthostatic hypotension.
- Exclusion of other conditions that could explain the symptoms.
- Persistent symptoms, generally for at least three months.

These criteria must be interpreted in the context of a complete clinical assessment. An elevated heart rate alone does not establish a diagnosis of POTS.

Clinical reference: Raj et al., Diagnosis and management of postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, CMAJ, 2022.

👩‍⚕️ Nursing Implications

Nurses can contribute to the recognition, assessment, and ongoing management of POTS.

Key areas include:

1. Assessment: Document symptoms, positional triggers, relevant history, and functional limitations.
2. Monitoring: Measure heart rate and blood pressure according to clinical protocols.
3. Patient safety: Assess dizziness, near-syncope, and fall risk.
4. Patient education: Explain symptom monitoring and individualized care plans.
5. Interdisciplinary care: Support communication among patients and healthcare professionals.
6. Evaluation: Monitor symptom burden, daily functioning, and response to the prescribed care plan.

Nursing interventions should be individualized and consistent with current clinical guidance.

📱 Digital Health Applications

Digital health tools may support POTS education, symptom tracking, and communication between patients and healthcare professionals.

1. Wearable Technology

Smartwatches and heart-rate monitors can help record physiological trends during daily activities.

However, wearable measurements are not diagnostic tests for POTS. Readings must be interpreted in context and should not replace clinical assessment.

2. Mobile Health Applications

A potential POTS symptom-tracking application could record:

- Heart rate and body position.
- Dizziness, fatigue, and palpitations.
- Activity and symptom patterns.
- Fluid intake when clinically relevant.
- Medication schedules and patient-reported responses.

Such tools may help patients communicate symptom patterns during appointments.

3. Artificial Intelligence and Data Analysis

Potential research applications include:

- Identifying symptom patterns in longitudinal datasets.
- Exploring associations between activity and reported symptoms.
- Improving the accessibility of health education.
- Supporting research into patient-reported outcomes.

AI-based healthcare tools require appropriate validation, privacy protections, and clinical oversight. They must not be presented as independently diagnosing POTS without adequate evidence.

4. Data Privacy and Ethics

Digital health projects should follow responsible data practices, including:

- Informed consent where applicable.
- Data minimization.
- Secure storage and transmission.
- Appropriate access controls.
- Protection of patient confidentiality.
- Compliance with applicable privacy and research requirements.

This repository does not collect patient data or provide an operational diagnostic system.

🦠 POTS and Long COVID

Autonomic symptoms can occur after SARS-CoV-2 infection, and POTS has received increased attention in discussions of post-COVID conditions.

However, not everyone with Long COVID has POTS, and POTS can occur without a history of COVID-19.

Further research is needed to understand the mechanisms, risk factors, and optimal management of post-infectious autonomic dysfunction.

Reference: "CDC Long COVID Clinical Guidance" (https://www.cdc.gov/long-covid/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)
